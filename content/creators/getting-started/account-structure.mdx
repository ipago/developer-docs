---
title: Accounts & projects
description: How accounts, projects, members, and permissions work on 2Pay.
---

2Pay is built around two core concepts: **accounts** and **projects**. Once the
difference clicks, organizing your stores and your team becomes simple. This page
explains both in depth, along with how members and permissions fit on top.

## Accounts

An **account represents a person**, you as an individual seller, or an employee of
a company. It is always tied to a single individual, never to a brand, a company,
or a storefront.

Your account holds:

- your login and personal identity,
- the projects you own or have been invited to,
- your personal preferences and notifications.

**One person, one account.** You can't hold two separate accounts, and an account
always maps to one real person. This keeps identity, access, and accountability
clean: every action in a project can be traced back to a specific person, and you
never have to juggle multiple logins.

If you work across several businesses or stores, you don't create extra accounts
for that. Instead, your single account gets access to multiple **projects**.

## Projects

A **project represents a single selling operation**, for example one game server,
one game, or one product line. Each project is fully self-contained and isolated
from the others. A project has its own:

- store and products (packages, bundles, subscriptions),
- branding, themes, and settings,
- payments, payouts, and billing,
- members and their permissions,
- API keys and integrations.

**One account can own or join many projects.** If you run several game servers,
each one is its own separate project, with separate catalogs, separate revenue,
and separate teams. Nothing leaks between them.

### Examples

- A creator running **two Minecraft servers** has **one account** and **two
  projects**, one per server. Each server has its own store, prices, and payouts.
- A **studio** publishing one game typically has **one project** for that game,
  shared by several team members in different roles.
- An agency managing stores for multiple clients keeps **one project per client**,
  each isolated, while inviting the right people into each.

## Members & permissions

A project isn't limited to its owner. You can invite other people, each using
**their own account**, into a project, and give them exactly the access they need.
You never share a login.

Common responsibilities you can delegate:

- **Billing**, manage the plan, invoices, and payment details.
- **Support**, handle buyer questions and order or delivery issues.
- **Store**, manage products, packages, pricing, and themes.
- **Payouts**, manage withdrawal methods and trigger payouts.
- **Members**, invite or remove people and set their permissions.
- **Developers**, manage API keys and integrations.

Permissions are **granular**. When you add someone to a project, you choose which
areas they can access and what they're allowed to do there. A billing manager
never needs to touch your store, and a support agent never sees your payouts.

| Permission area | What it controls |
| --- | --- |
| Store | Products, packages, bundles, pricing, themes |
| Billing | Plan, invoices, and payment method |
| Payouts | Withdrawal methods and payouts |
| Support | Buyer messages and order/delivery issues |
| Members | Inviting people and setting their permissions |
| Developers | API keys and integrations |

<Callout type="info">
  Invite teammates into a project instead of sharing your login. Everyone keeps
  their own account, and you can adjust or revoke their permissions at any time.
</Callout>

## In short

- **One person = one account.** Accounts map to people, not companies.
- **One account = many projects.** Each store or server is its own isolated
  project.
- **One project = many members.** Invite people with their own accounts and give
  each the exact permissions they need.
